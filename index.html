About

Who I am

I make large scientific workloads run faster and cheaper, and I build the checks that tell you when an AI system is wrong.

I am a computer scientist and Ph.D. candidate at the University of Missouri, working with Dr. Praveen Rao in the Scalable Data Science Lab. My thesis is about optimizing scientific workflows, mostly genomics and neuroimaging pipelines, on NSF cyberinfrastructure such as FABRIC and CloudLab. In practice that means multi-GPU scheduling, Slurm clusters, knowledge graphs over billions of statements, and a lot of benchmarking.

Since July 2026 I have been a research intern at Siemens Technology in Princeton, working on validation for agentic LLM pipelines. Before the Ph.D. I spent two years in statistical genomics at the International Rice Research Institute, where I built DeepMap and trained more than a hundred scientists. I have also shipped three iOS apps of my own.

Publications

  1. A Benchmark for Cost and Energy-Efficient Execution of Neuroimaging Workflows on Commodity Clusters

    A. Kumar, V. Omelyusik, S. Nair, P. Rao

    IEEE International Symposium on Workload Characterization (IISWC), Boulder, CO, 2026

    First authorConferenceNSF OAC-2417875
    Abstract

    We evaluate DeepPrep, a GPU-accelerated neuroimaging preprocessing pipeline, on six public datasets and four cluster configurations across makespan, power, energy, carbon and cost. The best configuration depends on the structural-to-functional scan size ratio; a CPU-GPU cluster cut energy by about 72% and power by 74% versus a high-core CPU cluster. We give a procedure for picking the cluster for a chosen target metric. Reproducibility artifact: DeepNeuroBench on GitHub.

  2. Paper on validating a production LLM-to-knowledge-graph pipeline

    A. Kumar and Siemens Technology co-authors

    CAIN 2027, industry track (co-located with ICSE) — under review

    First authorUnder reviewIndustry
    Summary

    A layered validation framework for an agentic LLM pipeline over building and HVAC maintenance data. It catches silent failures at stage boundaries instead of letting incorrect outputs through. Title and full author list to follow once the review concludes.

  3. Optimizing the Variant Calling Pipeline Execution on Human Genomes Using GPU-Enabled Machines

    A. Kumar, P. Rao, P. Sanders

    16th ACM International Conference on Bioinformatics, Computational Biology and Health Informatics (BCB Companion), Philadelphia, 2025

    First authorConference
    Abstract

    Combines machine learning runtime prediction with job-shop scheduling for human-genome variant calling on GPU machines using NVIDIA Parabricks, for an average 2× speedup over a greedy approach and 1.6× over a dynamic approach.

  4. A Scalable Tool for Analyzing Genomic Variants of Humans Using Knowledge Graphs and Graph Machine Learning

    S. Prasanna, A. Kumar, D. Rao, E. J. Simoes, P. Rao

    Frontiers in Big Data, vol. 7, 2025 · 6 citations

    JournalOpen access

  5. A Scalable Tool for Democratizing Variant Calling on Human Genomes Using Commodity Clusters

  6. Extreme Trait GWAS (Et-GWAS): Unraveling Rare Variants in the 3,000 Rice Genome

    N. Gnanapragasam, V. V. Prasanth, K. T. Sundaram, A. Kumar, B. Pahi, et al.
