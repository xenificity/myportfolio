Who I am

I make large scientific workloads run faster and cheaper, and I build the checks that tell you when an AI system is wrong.

I am a Ph.D. candidate in computer science at the University of Missouri, working with Dr. Praveen Rao in the Scalable Data Science Lab. My thesis is about optimizing scientific workflows, mostly genomics and neuroimaging pipelines, on NSF cyberinfrastructure such as FABRIC and CloudLab. In practice that means multi-GPU scheduling, Slurm clusters, knowledge graphs over billions of statements, and a lot of benchmarking.

Since July 2026 I have been a research intern in Siemens' Foundational Technology AI group in Princeton, designing a validation framework for agentic LLM pipelines. Before the Ph.D. I spent two years in statistical genomics at the International Rice Research Institute, where I shipped DeepMap and trained more than a hundred scientists. On weekends I ship small iOS apps.

Publications

  1. DeepNeuroBench: A Benchmark for Cost and Energy-Efficient Execution of Neuroimaging Workflows on Commodity Clusters

    A. Kumar, P. Rao, S. Nair, V. Omelyusik

    IEEE International Symposium on Workload Characterization (IISWC), Boulder, CO, 2026

    First authorConferenceNSF 2417875
    Abstract

    We evaluate DeepPrep, a GPU-accelerated neuroimaging preprocessing pipeline, on six public datasets and four cluster configurations across makespan, power, energy, carbon and cost. The best configuration depends on the structural-to-functional scan size ratio; a CPU+GPU cluster cut energy by about 72% and power by 74% versus a high-core CPU cluster. We give a procedure for picking the cluster for a chosen target metric. Reproducibility artifact on GitHub; paper forthcoming on IEEE Xplore.

  2. Layer-Boundary Validation for a Production LLM Pipeline: Experiences at Siemens

    A. Kumar, C. Budnik, et al.

    CAIN 2027, industry track (co-located with ICSE) — under review

    First authorUnder reviewIndustry
    Abstract

    A layered validation framework for an agentic LLM pipeline that extracts structured knowledge from industrial data. It catches silent failures at component boundaries and routes low-confidence outputs to human review rather than emitting incorrect results. Two related invention disclosures filed at Siemens Technology.

  3. Optimizing the Variant Calling Pipeline Execution on Human Genomes Using GPU-Enabled Machines

    A. Kumar, P. Rao, P. Sanders

    16th ACM Conference on Bioinformatics, Computational Biology and Health Informatics (ACM BCB), Philadelphia, 2025

    First authorConference
    Abstract

    A distributed multi-GPU pipeline for variant calling on COVID-19 human genomes using NVIDIA Parabricks, scheduled with a job-shop formulation (Google OR-Tools). Doubles throughput on FABRIC and CloudLab without extra hardware.

  4. A Scalable Tool for Analyzing Genomic Variants of Humans Using Knowledge Graphs and Graph Machine Learning

    S. Prasanna, A. Kumar, D. Rao, E. J. Simoes, P. Rao

  5. A Scalable Tool for Democratizing Variant Calling on Human Genomes Using Commodity Clusters
